# SER

As voice-assisted technologies continue to expand their presence across sectors such as healthcare, education, customer engagement, and human-computer interaction, Speech Emotion Recognition (SER) has become a key area of focus. However, accurately identifying emotions from speech is a complex task, largely due to inconsistencies in vocal tone, pronunciation, speech styles, and interference from background sounds [2]. While classical machine learning approaches like Support Vector Machines (SVM), Naïve Bayes, and Decision Trees have achieved some level of success, they often fall short in modeling the dynamic and sequential nature of speech. In contrast, recent progress in deep learning has paved the way for more effective solutions. Models such as Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), Long Short-Term Memory networks (LSTMs), and hybrid frameworks like CNN-LSTM and CNN-GRU have demonstrated strong capabilities in extracting meaningful spatial and temporal characteristics from audio signals.
